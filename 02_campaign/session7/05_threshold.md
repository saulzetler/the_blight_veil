## What This Scene Does

This is the session's other big swing: a scene that runs exactly like combat — initiative rolled once, discrete rounds, one action per character per turn — except nobody is attacking anything, and nothing here has hit points. Every "turn" is an offering: a truth, a memory, a piece of themselves, given to a site that Mira Ashwick already told the party (back in Session 5) is *"holding something it hasn't decided to let go of yet."* The mechanic exists to make that idea feel structural, not just narrated — the site is a participant with its own turn order, not a backdrop.

## Must-Hit Beats

> [!important] Must Hit
> **① The site is felt as pressure before it's understood as a trial.** Same family of sensation as the Listening Rock's pulse, but tighter, faster, and it doesn't release the way that one did.

> [!important] Must Hit
> **② Roll initiative once. Run three rounds. Every PC gets one turn per round — an offering, not an attack.** Full mechanic below.

> [!important] Must Hit
> **③ Nobody fails out of the scene.** The site always opens by the end of Round 3. What changes is the price. Protect the session's stated goal — the party reaches the resonance site tonight, full stop — while still making the outcome matter.

## Arriving at the Root

> [!quote] Read Aloud — The Clenched Root
> The ridgeline the map promised turns out to be less a ridge than a fist — the ground rises and closes inward, roots breaking the surface in overlapping arcs like knuckles. At the center, where the Listening Rock had a stone, this place has nothing visible at all. Just root, closing over root, closing over root, in a knot too deliberate to be growth and too old to be built.
>
> There's no stillness here the way the Listening Rock's clearing was still. If anything the air is *tighter* — a held breath instead of a released one. The pulse hits you before you're close enough to have expected it: fast, hard, under a minute between beats, nothing like the patient three-minute rhythm from before.
>
> Whatever this is, it has not made peace with anything.

Let the party approach, investigate, and discuss before the trial actually begins. Nothing triggers it automatically — it starts the moment someone makes real contact (touching the root-knot, stepping fully inside the ring of exposed roots, or a PC's attuned item reacting strongly enough that you decide it counts).

> [!quote] Read Aloud — The Trial Begins
> The moment [contact happens], the pressure doesn't build — it *arrives*, all at once, in your chest, your teeth, the soles of your feet. Somewhere underneath the knot of roots, something old and patient and *closed* turns its attention on all of you at exactly the same time.
>
> It doesn't ask permission. It asks a price.

**Roll initiative.** This sets speaking/acting order for the three rounds to come — nothing else.

---

## The Trial Mechanic

Run this exactly like a short combat, minus damage and hit points:

1. **Three rounds.** Each PC gets one turn per round, in initiative order.
2. **On their turn, a PC narrates one offering** — something true, something given up, something admitted — in response to a prompt (use the personalized prompts below, or improvise from what you know of their arc) — and makes **one skill check of their choice**, justified by the fiction, same DC-by-ambition logic as Session 6's No-Grid Mechanic (DC 12–16 based on how much they're actually giving).
3. **Track successes as a shared pool**, not per-character — every success (meeting or beating the DC) adds one token. Every failure adds one **Crack** instead.
4. **The site always opens after Round 3.** What differs is the cost — see Resolution.

> [!tip] If Maria came (Branch A from Scene 02)
> She doesn't take a turn in the trial — she's not who it's asking anything of — but she can tell the party, once, before Round 1 begins, roughly what it wants: *"It's not testing your strength. It never is. It's asking what you're willing to say out loud."* This is enough to let the table understand the mechanic without you breaking the fourth wall to explain it.

## What the Root Wants — Prompts By Character

Use these as the site's "ask" on each PC's first turn; let Rounds 2 and 3 escalate naturally from whatever they offered in Round 1, rather than scripting three separate prompts per person.

> [!quote] Zennor
> The root shows him nothing new — just the kitchen from his Session 6 dream, Eris, Sela, the twine. But this time it waits. It wants him to say, out loud, which version of the story he's been telling himself: that she killed their daughter, or that he doesn't actually know.
> *(A successful, honest check lets him offer the admission itself — "I don't know" — as the price. A failed or evasive check has the root take something physical instead: a thread of the pink twine bracelet unravels on its own and is gone.)*

> [!quote] Fergie
> The site doesn't show him Maria. It shows him the Porter Stone, warm, and asks — not in words — whether he came this far for the anomalies, or for her.
> *(A successful check lets him answer honestly, whichever way that goes, and the site accepts it. A failed check makes the stone run cold for the rest of the scene — not broken, just quiet, like it's embarrassed on his behalf.)*

> [!quote] Hog
> The root doesn't ask for a story. It asks him to say one true thing about himself with no joke attached to it — no bit, no deflection, nothing performed.
> *(This is deceptively hard for Hog specifically — track whether his player actually manages it without a punchline. A successful check is the honest line itself. A failed check costs him the tusk-trophy from Session 6, which is simply gone from his belt when the trial ends — small, almost funny, and exactly the kind of loss that lands harder than it should.)*

> [!quote] Oscar
> The same question from their Session 6 dream, asked directly this time instead of in sleep: *"What were you made to be? And is that still what you are?"*
> *(A successful check lets Oscar answer in their own words — whatever that means for their player. A failed check means the terrarium goes fully dark for one round — not damaged, just unresponsive — the single most alarming thing that's happened to it all campaign.)*

## Skill Checks — Reference DCs

| Approach | DC | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| A genuinely vulnerable, specific truth | 12 | The site rewards specificity over performance. |
| A true statement offered guardedly, hedged | 14 | Still honest, just costs more to say. |
| Deflection dressed up as honesty | 16 | The site can tell the difference. This is the "trying to get away with less" DC. |
| Offering an object or physical token instead of a truth | 13 | Valid, and sometimes the right call — not every PC needs to bare their soul three times in one scene. |

## Resolution

Count total successes across all three rounds (out of a possible 12, if all four PCs act all three rounds):

- **9–12 successes — Clean.** The root opens without extracting anything further. Whatever was offered, was enough. Move straight to Scene 06's read-aloud.
- **5–8 successes — Costly, but survivable.** The root opens, but one Crack becomes permanent — pick whichever failed offering landed hardest at the table and let it stick (the unraveled bracelet thread stays gone, the terrarium takes a few extra seconds to "wake" from now on, etc.). Narrate it as a small, real loss, not a mechanical penalty.
- **0–4 successes — It takes what it wants.** The root opens anyway — the party is not locked out, full stop — but every failed offering's cost becomes real and permanent, and additionally, whoever had the fewest successes personally finds their attuned item doesn't work quite the same way again (DM's call on the specific mechanical effect, kept minor — flavor over crippling).

> [!warning] DM Only
> No matter the outcome, protect this: **the party gets into the site tonight.** This scene is about what it costs, never about whether it happens. If the table is having a rough go of the rolls, lean on narrative framing to keep tension high without threatening the session's actual goal.
