## Running the Scene

> [!quote] Read Aloud — Down and In
> The passage the root opened is narrow enough that you go single file, root-wall close enough to touch on both sides, warm the way the tally-mark tunnel was warm, but wronger — less like proximity to fire and more like proximity to something that used to be fire and forgot how to stop.
>
> It isn't long. Thirty feet, maybe forty, and then it opens.
>
> The chamber is small — smaller than the Hollow Nave, smaller than you expected anything this guarded to be. The compression Mira described isn't a feeling anymore. It's architecture. The roots don't just surround this space, they've grown *into* it, over decades, wrapping close the way a fist closes over something it's afraid to drop.
>
> And at the center, held in a lattice of root and something that isn't quite root — a shape.
>
> A person. Or was. Or still is, depending on how you want to measure it.

Give the table a moment here before the next beat — let them look, ask questions, approach.

> [!quote] Read Aloud — The Figure
> From the shoulders down, more crystal than flesh now — translucent, faintly lit from somewhere inside it, the way the Choir's petrification is described in the oldest, most heretical Ashroot rumors nobody in this party has any reason to have heard yet. The face is the last part still recognizably a face, eyes closed, expression unreadable — not peaceful, not in pain, just *paused*, the way a held breath is paused.
>
> The chest rises. Falls. Slowly. Barely. But it does.
>
> One hand — the only part of the figure still fully flesh — rests loosely closed at their side.
>
> Around the wrist: a bracelet. Pink twine, badly made, the kind of knotwork a child does when they're proud of something that didn't quite come out right. Frayed at the edges. Fused, at the very last inch, into the crystal creeping up the arm.

Say nothing further about what this means. If a player asks a direct question ("Is that — whose is that?"), answer only what a character could physically observe, and nothing more:

- *"It's twine. It's old. It's tied the way a child ties things, not the way an adult would."*
- *"You can't tell how long it's been there. Longer than recently."*
- *"The figure doesn't react to your voice, your touch, or your light. Nothing about them changes."*

If someone tries to free the figure, wake them, or use the terrarium on them — let the attempt happen, but do not resolve it. The pulse spikes, hard, once — the fastest and hardest it's been all scene — and that's where you cut.

## Ending the Session

> [!quote] Closing Line
> The pulse hits once more — faster than before, sharper — and in the half-second of light it throws across the chamber, every one of you sees the same thing at once: the twine bracelet, catching the light exactly the way it would if it were still being worn by someone who could feel it.
>
> Then the light settles. The figure doesn't move.
>
> *End of Session 7.*

## If the Party Won't Stop

> [!warning] DM Only — likeliest ways the cut gets fought
> Same principle as Session 6's cliffhanger: hold the line with an in-fiction answer wherever you can, rather than a flat DM ruling.

| If they... | Say |
|---|---|
| Try to break the crystal | It doesn't chip, crack, or respond to any damage you're currently capable of dealing. This is not a puzzle solvable tonight. |
| Try to identify the figure with magic (detect thoughts, etc.) | Nothing comes through — not blocked, exactly, more like reaching for a radio station that isn't broadcasting on this frequency right now. |
| Demand you confirm who it is | *"You don't know. Not yet. That's where we're stopping."* Said kindly, but said. |
| Try to stay the night at the site | Fine — cut to black on the party settling in, still watching the figure, still not sure what they've found. |
