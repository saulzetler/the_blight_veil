## What This Scene Does

The first real fight since the airship — and unlike Session 6's deliberately bloodless "nobody dies today" rule, this one has actual stakes. It should be short: two soldiers, no reinforcements arriving mid-fight, over in three or four rounds. The point isn't difficulty, it's texture — confirming Thane's forces are actively working the Ashroot, not just sitting over Rootspire, and giving the party a real choice about how they handle Concord soldiers who are, this time, just doing a job.

---

## Must-Hit Beats

> [!important] Must Hit
> **① This is a two-person forward patrol, not an ambush squad.** They're canvassing, not hunting the party specifically — Secret #1 in the master outline. Let a perceptive player realize this mid-fight if they ask the right question.

> [!important] Must Hit
> **② Give the party a real choice about lethality.** Subdue, kill, or let them flee are all valid, all consequential, and none of them is the "correct" answer. This campaign isn't black-and-white — neither are these two.

> [!important] Must Hit
> **③ Whatever happens, they were carrying something worth finding.** See Aftermath.

## The Encounter

Two Concord soldiers, stat blocks from `03_characters/npcs/concord_patrol.md`, used as-written:

- **Concord Scout** (CR 2) — ranged, mobile, will try to sound the alarm early if given the chance.
- **Concord Warrior** (CR 2) — melee, shield, holds a line rather than chasing.

**No Captain Kael, no Lantern Line, no reinforcements.** This is deliberately a small fight — two mid-tier soldiers a level-4–5 party of four should handle without serious risk, especially with any tactical advantage (surprise, terrain, magic prepared).

> [!warning] DM Only — Tuning
> If your table is higher or lower than level 4–5, adjust HP by ±20% rather than adding creatures — the point is a short, clean fight, not an attrition battle.

## Running the Fight

> [!quote] Read Aloud — Contact
> The trees thin just enough to see them before they see you — two Concord soldiers, road-dusted, standing over a rough charcoal sketch pinned to a tree trunk with a knife. One is young, maybe younger than he's trying to look. The other has the specific tiredness of someone who has done this exact job more times than he can count.
>
> The younger one spots you first. His hand goes to his side — not fast, not a killing reach, just the reflex of someone who's been told to be ready.
>
> *"Stand down and this doesn't have to—"*

Let the party decide whether this starts as combat, negotiation, or an ambush of their own. If they try to talk first:

> **Concord Scout:** *"We're not here for you specifically. Wish I could say we were the only ones out here, but I can't."*
> **Concord Warrior:** *(cutting him off, hard)* *"Quiet."* *(To the party:)* *"Orders are orders. Doesn't mean I enjoy it."*

If the Scout gets a turn before combat is joined and isn't stopped, he tries to sound his signal horn (per the stat block's Signal Horn trait) — this doesn't summon reinforcements this scene (there are none nearby), but it means the patrol's disappearance will eventually be noticed. Interrupting it (grapple, shove, a fast spell) prevents even that.

**Run this as a normal, gridded or theater-of-the-mind combat** — full initiative, full rounds, actual HP and damage. This is the one place tonight that *is* what it looks like.

## Aftermath

Whatever the outcome, the patrol was carrying:

- A **folded map** — the same four anomaly sites Maria has marked, in a different hand, plus a **fifth site circled off the western edge of the paper**, unlabeled. Neither map agrees on exactly where the fifth site is.
- **Standard Vision-branch orders**, dense and bureaucratic, confirming they're one of several two-soldier teams "canvassing designated resonance loci, eastern Ashroot, report irregularities only" — Secret #1, confirmed in writing if the party reads it.
- If the Warrior survives and is questioned rather than killed, he'll answer plainly, without much resistance — he's tired, not devoted. He knows the fourth site is "closed," per the standing briefing — every team that's been sent to it has come back with nothing to report, which is itself considered a notable result.
- If the Scout survives, he's more nervous and more talkative — Secret #2. Under any gentle pressure he'll admit he doesn't fully understand what he's looking for, only that his instruments haven't reacted at any of the sites he's checked so far.

> [!tip] Optional — if the party lets one or both go
> A soldier released rather than killed remembers it. This doesn't need to pay off this session — file it as a small, real kindness (or a mistake) the world will eventually respond to.

---

## If the Party Avoids the Fight Entirely

A sufficiently stealthy or clever approach (high group Stealth, a good idea backed by a roll) can let the party observe the patrol, take the map off them unnoticed, or simply route around. This is a completely valid outcome — the fight is here to be interesting if it happens, not mandatory as written. If they avoid it, they still find a discarded, weather-worn copy of the same four-site map pinned to a tree nearby (the "sketch pinned with a knife" from the read-aloud), minus the fifth site — that detail only comes from searching the soldiers directly.
