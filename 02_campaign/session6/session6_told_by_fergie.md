# THE BLIGHT VEIL

## Session 6 — As Told by Fergie

_Fire Genasi. Cleric. Recently informed I am, quote, "the fire guy."_

---

Okay. So. A lot happened. I'm going to tell it properly, because somebody has to, and Zennor's version would just be forty minutes of sighing.

We were still down by the water when it started — Zennor and his handprint, warm under his palm, pointing out across the river like it wanted him to follow it in. I checked my Porter Stone against it, because that's what I do now, that's my whole thing, and it gave me nothing. Fine. Not everything is for me. I'm learning that. Growth.

And then the sky just — stopped being a sky.

You know that feeling when a room goes quiet because someone important walked in? Rootspire did that, except the someone important was an entire airship, iron ribs, cold white sigils glowing off its belly like it had swallowed a piece of the Concord and was wearing the glow like a coat. And standing at the rail, looking down at all of us like we were something he'd already decided about: a man built out of scars he'd chosen to keep unpolished. High Marshal Varric Thane. He said our names without saying our names — *those responsible for the death of a Concord Captain* — and I thought, well. That's us. That's definitely us. We did do that.

He gave the whole city one warning, and then he gave us a fireball, twenty metres from where we were standing, just to prove he wasn't bluffing. *"This is not the attack. This is merely the knock on the door."* I've heard worse pickup lines, honestly.

Here's the part I'm proud of: everyone panicked, the whole market just — went, and I didn't. I called it. Down, into the roots, the way we already knew. Eighteen on the persuasion check, which I will be mentioning again, possibly forever. A good chunk of the crowd came with me. Not all of them. Zennor tried to talk Timoor down — Timoor, our own Timoor, who has been with us since the tavern — and it went so badly he bolted off in the opposite direction, shouting that we were the problem. Which — rude, from a man we personally vouched for, but I understand the impulse, my week hasn't been great either. We don't actually know where he ended up.

Down in the tunnels, cornered by our own former fan club, we did the thing where you stop trying to be reasonable and just become slightly terrifying instead. Zennor pulled his blades. I lit my hands up. Hog turned into an enormous toad — we vetoed lion, too predatory, we're trying to look powerful, not hungry — and it worked. They backed off. Somebody actually said, out loud, *we know the Concord is the real enemy,* which, yes. Thank you. Took you a fireball to figure that out but we'll take it.

Then a boy slid out of the dark like he'd been part of the wall the whole time and offered to get us out. Briar. Briar Fenstep. Oscar kissed his hand, because Oscar does that now, it's a whole bit, and Briar just let it happen, bless him. He knew a hidden way through — some contact of his, connected to the Cindersong, the same people who got us out of Orrenval in the first place. We told him we were part of it too and something in him just eased. He wouldn't give us the friend's name. I respect that more than I'd admit to his face. We promised to keep his secret. Small mercies, passed hand to hand.

The tunnel he put us in was warm and marked wall to wall with tally scratches. Four hundred and fifty-two of them, if you're counting, and somebody was. There was a kid's drawing near the floor — a tree, a sun, and something above the tree that nobody could name but everybody was thinking about, given the week we're having. Zennor got quiet looking at it. I didn't push. Some things you let a person sit with.

We came out into the open forest around dusk, walked for hours, made camp when we couldn't push further. I sent Hog and Oscar off to find food, mostly because I wanted five minutes where nobody needed anything from me, and got treated to the sound of Oscar having a full, doomed conversation with a warthog that ended in the warthog charging and me moonbeaming it out of pure reflex. It came back beautifully cooked, which I'm choosing to take credit for.

Around the fire we played Truth or Dare, and it turned into something more honest than the game usually earns. Zennor talked about his wife and his daughter — actually talked, not just went quiet and stared at the flames like usual — and caught himself mid-toast correcting who he was actually doing all this for. Not her. Sela. His girl. Hog told us about being sent away from home as a kid to train instead of following his father into the forge, and how he's spent years not knowing whether to be angry about it. I didn't say much. I just listened. That's new for me too, if I'm honest.

That night I dreamed about her before I ever saw her face.

Someone in the dark, unseen, studying me the way you'd study your own reflection before deciding it's really you. A voice, close: *I've been looking for you.* I woke up with my hand already closed around my Porter Stone, and it was warm — properly warm, like it had been having a much longer conversation without me.

And then the next morning, we found her standing in a garden like she'd been waiting there her entire life for this exact moment.

She looks like me. Not exactly — her flame runs cold, blue instead of whatever colour I run — but the bones of it are the same, the same instinct pointed two different directions. She said it before I could: *"I've been looking for you."* Same dream. Same night. I introduced myself properly — full name, the works, I wasn't going to waste an entrance like that — and she told me hers. Maria. Maria Zurita.

She's Concord-trained. Everyone clocked it immediately, and I won't pretend that didn't put a knot in my chest, because I've spent this whole journey trying to become something the Concord didn't make me into, and here's a woman who looks like my reflection and owes everything she knows to the people I ran from. But she's not *for* them. Not exactly. She made that distinction like it mattered to her, like she'd had to make it before, to herself, more than once.

She let me into a private room and taught me something nobody at my own temple ever would've — that fire and cold aren't opposites, they're the same instinct pointed two ways, and if you hold both on purpose neither one gets to surprise you. She showed me a stone of her own, black, holding something like a void in it — held next to mine, I could almost see through into her. She called what I learned The Inverted Ember. I can feel it sitting in my chest now, a colder thread running next to the warm one, waiting for me to need it.

But it's what she said after, once the hour we'd given her ran out, that I haven't stopped turning over since.

*"There is an echo. And my goal is to stabilize it. But if the echo cannot be stabilized, it must be severed."*

She couldn't explain it properly — a calling, a reflection, another presence that isn't quite separate from her. And when she said it, I felt something too. Faint. Mine to admit or not admit, I haven't decided yet.

She mentioned a name — Dawnswell — like it was something she'd half-forgotten and didn't want to fully remember. A Vision program. Constructs, built. Like Oscar. She thought it was dead. I don't think it is.

We ended the night playing the harmonic instruments together, properly this time, meaning it — and it landed. Something in the room actually aligned. Everyone felt it.

I don't know what she is to me yet. A mirror. A warning. Family, maybe, in whatever way that word means anything out here. But I know I felt her before I saw her, and I know whatever's inside her that needs stabilizing or severing is the kind of thing I'd rather understand than run from.

That's where we left it. Her, mid-sentence. Me, still holding a stone that's never been this warm before.

More soon, presumably. There's always more.
