## DM Prep Notes

> [!warning] DM Only
> - **No random encounters tonight.** Mirror the same principle Session 5 used for the descent — the danger here is psychological and emotional, not tactical. Protect the quiet.
> - **This scene exists to build tension toward Scene 05, not resolve anything.** The personal beats and the Night Visions are foreshadowing, not payoff — don't let any of them fully explain themselves tonight.
> - **The Luck Roll mechanic is from `04_reference/mechanics/rumors_mechanic.md`** — reuse it as written, just retheme the flavor text around today's events in Rootspire.
> - **The Night Visions are not a shared event.** Each PC dreams alone, about their own thing. Don't let the table cross-talk about them mid-scene — deliver each one privately (a note, a quiet aside, or just lowering your voice for that player's beat) and let the reactions surface at breakfast instead, if at all.
> - **Fergie's vision must not name or show Maria's face.** Scene 05's "seen and named for the first time" beat depends on this being the party's actual first sight of her. The dream should feel unmistakable in hindsight without spoiling it in the moment.
> - Keep this scene's total time under 30 minutes. It's runway into Scene 05, not a destination in itself.

## Running the Scene

> [!quote] Read Aloud — Making Camp
> You find a place to stop as the light goes properly gold and then grey — a hollow between root-humps, sheltered enough for a fire that won't be seen far. The Ashroot at night is a different animal than the Ashroot by day: quieter, but not silent. Something is always moving just past where your firelight reaches.
>
> Nobody says much at first. There's a lot to not say much about.

> [!tip] Oscar's Sensor — Decibel Reading
> **24 dB.** Fire crackle, insects, the occasional distant something. Ordinary background for a forest at night — nothing here for Oscar to flag, which is itself worth him saying out loud once, so the table has a baseline before the Night Visions section below breaks that baseline.

Let the party set watch order, handle mundane camp logistics briefly, then move into the Luck Roll.

## The Luck Roll

Have each player roll the existing d20 Luck Roll from `04_reference/mechanics/rumors_mechanic.md`. Theme every result around word spreading through Rootspire about today.

**1–10 (bad rumor):**
- *"They say the party brought that ship down on us on purpose."*
- *"Word's already reaching the next town over — travelers are being asked if they've seen strange metal men or fire-touched women on the road."*
- *"Someone's saying they saw [PC name] use forbidden magic in the square, right before the shots fired."*

**11–20 (good rumor):**
- *"Someone's saying the party stood between the crowd and the ship when the fire came down."*
- *"A Rootspire local is quietly spreading word that the Marshal is the real threat, not the outsiders."*
- *"There's talk that [PC name] saved a child in the chaos — nobody's sure it's true, but it's spreading."*

> [!warning] DM Only
> Let a rumor from this roll matter later in the campaign — a Concord patrol on the road who's heard the bad version, or a stranger further out who's already heard the good version and treats the party differently because of it. Note whichever rolls land tonight; you'll want them.

*These are already-seeded threads from the Act 1 roadmap (`01_worldbuilding/Act1/act1_roadmap.md`), relocated into this outdoor session since it's the natural place for them to land. Keep each brief — a flicker, not a scene.*

> [!tip] Hog

> [!tip] Zennor
> His amulet goes cold at the edge of camp, for no reason anyone can name. It stays cold for the rest of the watch. He's felt this particular cold before, and he doesn't like what it usually means.

> [!tip] Oscar
> The terrarium leans, faintly, in the direction they're travelling — the same orienting pull it had in the lower roots, quieter now, more patient. It's not urgent. It's *interested.*

> [!tip] Fergie
> His Porter Stone double-pulses once in the dark — one warm beat, one cold — and is still again before he can be sure he felt it. If `EXT_heat_without_flame` already ran, this reads as confirmation: whatever's ahead is the same thing that left the cold-centred handprint. The bleed is getting closer.

## If a Player Investigates Their Own Beat

> [!tip] Optional — only if a player pushes past "it happens, nobody explains it"
> None of these should resolve anything — hold the line at "unsettling, not answered." They're a hook, not a reveal.

- **Hog**, if he examines the gauntlet token closely: it's warm to the touch, faintly, in a way it's never been before — like something on the other end just noticed him back.
- **Zennor**, if he asks the amulet (or the party) what the cold means: he doesn't have an answer, only a memory-shape of a feeling — the same cold he felt the night his daughter was taken. He won't say that part out loud unless his player chooses to.
- **Oscar**, if they try to communicate with the terrarium or ask it something directly: no words, no clearer signal — just the lean, patient and unbothered, like being asked a question by something that doesn't experience urgency the way they do.
- **Fergie**, if he tries to reach back through the Stone: nothing answers. It's not silence like *absence* — it's silence like someone on the other end is listening very carefully and choosing not to respond yet.

## The Night Visions

> [!warning] DM Only
> Run these once everyone is actually asleep — after watch order is set, after the Luck Roll, after the banter dies down. Deliver each one privately to the relevant player if you can manage it (lean in, drop your voice, hand them a note) — these are not for the table to workshop together in real time. Let the *players* compare notes over breakfast if they want to; don't force it. None of these resolve anything. They plant.

### Fergie — The Voice Without a Face

> [!quote] Read (privately) to Fergie's player
> You're warm, and then you're not. The fire you fell asleep next to is gone — not out, just *elsewhere* — and in its place is a cold that doesn't hurt so much as it *waits*.
>
> There's someone in the dream with you. You don't see her. You feel her the way you feel your own Porter Stone — a pressure, a pulse, a shape where a person should be. There's no threat in it, and her attention isn't quite on you either — more on the *space* where you are, the way you'd study a reflection before deciding it was really you.
>
> Something in your chest answers something in hers before you've decided whether you want it to.
>
> A voice, very close, that might be inside the dream and might be inside the Stone:
> *"I've been looking for you."*
>
> You wake with your hand already closed around the Porter Stone, and it's warm — properly warm, not the double-pulse from before — like it's been having a much longer conversation than you were invited to.

> [!warning] DM Only — Fergie
> Do not let this dream show her face, name her, or describe her flame in enough detail to be recognizable before Scene 05. The line *"I've been looking for you"* is the exact line Maria says in person — that's the point. A sharp player will feel the déjà vu land like a gut-punch when she says it for real. Don't explain the overlap. Let them get there.

### Oscar — The Chamber That Isn't Empty

> [!quote] Read (privately) to Oscar's player
> You're back in the Hollow Nave. Not remembering it — *in* it, the way dreams put you back inside a room instead of showing it to you.
>
> The crystalline housing is where you left it, in Vessel-Null's opened chest. But this time it's not inert. It's turning, slowly, the way the terrarium turned to face it — except now it's turning to face *you*, and you understand, the way you only understand things in dreams, that it has been doing this the entire time you weren't looking.
>
> The obscured letters on the housing — D, W, L, and the numeral you couldn't finish reading — rearrange themselves for just a moment into something almost legible. You don't catch it. It's gone before you can be sure it was ever there.
>
> Somewhere below all of it, three minutes apart, something pulses. Once. Slowly. The same rhythm as the Listening Rock.
>
> A question, in a voice that sounds like it's borrowing from both you and Vessel-Null at once:
> *"What were you made to be? And is that still what you are?"*
>
> You wake with the terrarium held against your chest — you don't remember picking it up — and it's leaning, hard, in the direction the party is walking. Harder than it ever has before.

> [!tip] Oscar's Sensor — Decibel Reading
> **No source detected.** Oscar heard the pulse and the question as clearly as anything he's ever heard — but the sensor logged nothing. No external sound entered his audio intake at any point during the dream. Whatever he heard, it didn't arrive through his ears.

> [!warning] DM Only — Oscar
> This is a continuation of Session 5's Scene 04 (`session5/04_revelation.md`) and Scene 05 (`session5/05_resonance.md`) threads — the housing, the tuning-fork resonance, the Dawnswell designation. Don't resolve the letters or the question. The terrarium's harder lean is a genuine mechanical/narrative signal that Maria's house is Veil-adjacent — treat it as confirmation, not new information, if Oscar shared the housing discovery with the party. The sensor's null reading is a deliberately unsettling detail — the "sound" bypassed his normal hearing entirely, which is worth letting the player sit with.

### Hog — The Thunderwood Invitational

> [!quote] Read (privately, and let it get a laugh) to Hog's player
> You're in a fighting pit. Not a real one — the ring is made of storm-cloud, the ropes are lightning that hums when you touch them, and the crowd is every Thunderwood who ever lived, stacked up in the stands in order of how beefy they were in life. They are, without exception, extremely beefy.
>
> Across from you: an ancestor. Bigger than you. Obviously, insultingly bigger than you. He doesn't say anything — Thunderwoods apparently don't need to in the beyond — he just cracks his knuckles like distant thunder and grins the exact grin you've seen in the old tribe portraits.
>
> The bell — an actual, enormous bell — rings.
>
> You go in exactly the way you always go in: no plan, all fists, drawstring already a little loose from the enthusiasm of the running start. And exactly like that first fight back home, at the worst possible moment, your pants drop.
>
> This time, though, the crowd doesn't gawk. They *roar.* Your ancestor actually staggers back a step, thrown by the sheer audacity of it, and in the half-second opening you land a punch square in his jaw that sounds like the bell going off a second time.
>
> He goes down. The stands lose their minds. Somewhere in the noise, one voice — you can't tell whose, but it sounds proud in a way that feels very old — shouts:
> *"THAT'S NOT A FLAW, THAT'S A TECHNIQUE!"*
>
> You wake up grinning for no reason you can explain out loud, the storm-sigil on your gauntlet token warm under your thumb, like it's still buzzing from the crowd noise.

> [!warning] DM Only — Hog
> Pure texture, not a mystery — this one's allowed to just be fun. If you want a thread out of it, the sigil being warm (versus its earlier unexplained flare in the Personal Beats section above) is a small, cumulative signal that something ancestral is actively responding to Hog specifically, tonight — save the payoff for whenever his arc doc (`hog_arc.md`) calls for it.

### Zennor — The Twine and the Fire

> [!quote] Read (privately, and give it room — this one should land heavier than the others) to Zennor's player
> It's warm before it's anything else. A kitchen you don't remember ever standing in, except you do — every detail of it, the chip in the second bowl, the way the light came in low through one window in the evening. Eris is there, looking at you the way a wife looks at a husband who's finally home — none of the stranger's caution yet, none of the lie underneath it either.
>
> Your daughter is on the floor, small hands working a loop of pink twine into something that will never quite become a bracelet no matter how many times she starts over. She holds it up anyway, proud of the failure.
>
> *"Look what I made you."*
>
> You reach for it. Your hand closes on the twine — and the kitchen is gone. It's cold, and it's dark, and there's a fire that gives off no heat, black at the center and orange only at the very edges, and Eris's voice is coming from somewhere inside it, except it isn't her voice anymore by the time it finishes the sentence:
>
> *"Look what I made you."*
>
> You wake with the twine bracelet in a death-grip and the amulet ice-cold against your chest — colder than the watch-beat earlier tonight, cold enough that for a second you're not sure it's ever going to warm up again.

> [!warning] DM Only — Zennor
> Do not resolve whether this is memory, the amulet's influence, or something reaching for him from wherever Eris/the daughter actually are — that ambiguity is the point, and it's live territory for `zennor_arc.md` and `amulet_v2.md`'s later stages. The voice-shift at the end (Eris's line finishing in a different register) deliberately echoes the amulet lore's established detail that he sometimes hears her voice shade into his daughter's, or vice versa — don't over-explain it in the moment; let it sit as dread.
