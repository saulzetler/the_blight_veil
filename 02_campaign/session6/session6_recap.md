## Session 6 Recap

_The Sky Opens · Flight Through the Roots · Maria Zurita's House_

## The Handprint at the Waterline

Picking up from Session 5's close: Zennor is still fixed on the handprint by the river — the one he's convinced belongs to his wife, or his daughter, or both. It's warm to the touch, child-sized by his read, and it points toward the water with nothing else nearby to explain it — no companion prints, no trail.

The party checks their open threads before committing to anything: the main thread (reach the Ashroot, investigate the four anomalies) and the side quests — the unidentified construct, the harmonic assessment team the messenger Pell warned them about, and Zennor's own hunt for the flame-resistant woman rumored in the eastern roots. The assessment team's letter said "three days out" — written a few days ago now, which means the real number could be smaller.

Fergie pulls out his Porter Stone to check it against the handprint. Nothing. Zennor checks **Jaihroquai**, the Council's Anomaly Reliquary — it's pointing the same direction it's pointed since they surfaced, unmoved by the handprint specifically.

Then the light changes.

---

## The Sky Opens

The canopy dims like something has moved between Rootspire and the sun. It takes a second for eyes to adjust — and then the shapes resolve: giant iron ribs, a vast hull, and low across its belly, the cold glowing sigil of a Concord seal. An airship. Nobody in the party — nobody in Rootspire — has ever seen one this close.

A voice, amplified by something none of them can name, reaches the whole city at once. At the ship's rail: a figure a head taller than anyone near him, close-cropped grey hair, a jaw broken and reset more than once, pale eyes fixed on the city and nowhere else. Ceremonial steel scored with old, deliberately unpolished battle marks. A banner in flame-orange thread hangs from his shoulder, heavy enough to barely move in a wind that's moving everything else.

> **High Marshal Varric Thane:** *"People of Rootspire. I am High Marshal Varric Thane, Warden of the Concordant Armies. This city harbors those responsible for the death of a Concord Captain — and for the defiance of a lawful trial before your own Council. You will surrender them, or you will be treated as complicit."*
>
> *"You have been warned once. That is more mercy than the Trifarix usually extends."*

The party is standing in the open market when it happens, right by the riverbank — impossible to pretend this isn't about them. A round of half-serious scheming follows (disguises, claiming to be from out of town, briefly floating the idea of offering up **Timoor** — their tavern-met companion since Session 4, called over to weigh in and immediately regretting it) before the ship makes the point moot: a ball of flame arcs down and detonates roughly twenty metres from the market. Nobody dies, but the shockwave shatters the weaker stalls. Thane's voice follows it, amused and merciless:

> **Varric Thane:** *"This is not the attack. This is merely the knock on the door."*

The city breaks into panic. Roll initiative — not for a fight, just to sort out who acts first in the chaos.

---

## Flight Into the Roots

**Fergie** goes first and makes the call: down, into the Deep Roots, the tunnel network the party already knows from earlier sessions. He shouts it to the crowd as much as to the party and rolls an 18 Persuasion — a good chunk of the fleeing crowd follows him and Hog toward the entrance.

**Hog** tries to physically herd Timoor along with them, and **Oscar** ushers him too, appealing on safety grounds: *"It is not safe to shelter in place. You must shelter underground. Come on, Timoor."* **Zennor** tries to talk him down further and rolls a critical failure — Timoor bolts in the wrong direction entirely, and it's loud enough that a portion of the fleeing crowd turns hostile instead of grateful, deciding out loud that the party are the reason any of this is happening and refusing to follow them further.

A second fireball lands near the retreating group — again no casualties, but it's the last straw for the wavering part of the crowd. Some still follow the party down. Others don't.

Underground, cornered in the tunnels with survivors who followed them, the mood turns. One of the locals rounds on the party: *"Why should we help you? You've brought ruin upon a great city. It's been so long since anything terrible happened in Rootspire."* Someone draws a blade. The party doesn't fight back — they lean into being frightening instead. Zennor draws his blades, Fergie sparks his hands alight, Hog wildshapes partway into something enormous and toad-shaped (a compromise — Oscar vetoes "lion," reasoning that a lion reads as a threat and they're trying to look formidable, not predatory). Three intimidation checks land (14, 16, 15) and the crowd backs off, conceding the obvious: *"We know the Concord is the real enemy."* One holdout still doesn't know the way further out.

---

## Briar Fenstep and the Cindersong Path

Someone slides out of the dark, seemingly from nowhere — a young man, well-worn, with the look of a scout rather than a threat. *"There's a way out from here."* He offers to lead them, and the party — plus whichever civilians are still with them — follow.

He gives his name as **Briar Fenstep**. Oscar greets him by kissing his hand, which Briar accepts with the tired grace of someone who has decided not to ask. He finds a branch in the tunnel wall that looks deliberately out of place — the first one Hog tries snaps uselessly in his hand — but Briar's own pull opens a hidden passage deeper into the roots.

Conversation on the way down reveals more than Briar means to give up. Pushed on how he knows this route, he admits he learned it from an associate connected to the **Cindersong** — the same underground network that smuggled the party out of Orrenval back in Session 1. He didn't expect the party to already know the name. Once they confirm they're part of "the Rebellion" too, Briar visibly relaxes, though he won't name his contact — he's not willing to put a friend's life in the party's hands on a first meeting.

The party promises not to expose him. In exchange, he answers what he can: the Lower Eastern Roots — where the rumored flame-resistant woman was last sighted — are much further out than the party assumed. Rootspire's root system runs for kilometres in every direction; getting there on foot is a matter of days, not hours. His own business finished, Briar asks to be excused to check on his own people in the city, and the party lets him go.

---

## Four Hundred and Fifty-Two Marks

The tunnel beyond is well-lit — almost painfully so, once every party member's own light source is added to it — and warm, smelling faintly of old smoke. The walls are covered in tally marks, hundreds of them. A careful count puts the total at **452**. Lower down, at knee height, a child's drawing: a tree, a sun, and a shape above the tree canopy that isn't a bird. Nobody can say for certain what it is. Given what's currently sitting in the sky over Rootspire, more than one person at the table has the same thought.

Zennor is asked, gently, whether any of this could be his family's — the marks, the drawing. The honest answer is that he doesn't know. He's lost track of how long it's actually been; somewhere in "a multitude of years," never more precise than that.

Further along, the tunnel opens into a stocked rest area — seating, food stores, access to a reservoir. Clearly used, and used somewhat recently, by someone who isn't the party. The tally count (452, and rising by one somewhere off-page) is left as an open thread — whoever is counting days down here hasn't stopped.

---

## Out of the Roots

The tunnel surfaces at a root-path leading up into the forest, well outside Rootspire proper. It's early evening. Oscar's built-in compass confirms the party is heading east — the same general direction as one of Jaihroquai's four anomaly readings. Looking back, the party can still see the Great Tree, though the airship itself has moved out of sight.

They walk roughly three hours, seven to ten in the evening, along a single path through increasingly open woodland, the root-highway beneath them tapering as it goes. By ten p.m. they're at the edge of exhaustion and call it — a long rest, rather than pushing on and taking exhaustion points.

---

## Camp: Rumor, Warthog, and Truth or Dare

They find a hollow between root-hubs, sheltered enough to risk a small fire. Oscar volunteers to take the full watch rather than sleep — they don't strictly need it, though they still have to power down partially and lose full mobility during the rest.

A luck roll for the night comes back at 13 — good news. Word is already moving through Rootspire that the party stood up to the Marshal, that some of them saved locals during the panic, and that at least one bystander spoke out against Thane in the chaos. Whatever the airship intended, the rumor mill is not entirely on the Concord's side.

**Fergie** sends Hog and Oscar out to hunt for food. Oscar, despite having no actual ability to speak with animals, has a full conversation with a cornered warthog anyway — informs it politely that it's been chosen to die, and is informed just as politely that the warthog would prefer not to. It charges; Fergie moonbeams it from a distance mid-fight, and between the radiant damage and the charge attack gone wrong, the warthog goes down. Oscar carries the (perfectly, if unintentionally, cooked) body back to camp. Hog harvests a tusk with more enthusiasm than technique — half the skull comes with it — and ties the whole head to his belt as a trophy.

Around the fire, a round of Truth or Dare turns into genuine backstory:

- **Zennor**, prompted about his "troubled soul," admits there is one — grief for his wife and daughter, worn smooth by years of the road. Mid-toast to becoming "a new man," he catches himself and corrects the table: it's his daughter he means to become better for, not his wife. He keeps her bracelet close and prays over it before sleep.
- **Hog** shares that his family sent him away young to train as a monk, away from his father's blacksmith trade — a choice he's spent years cycling through anger, confusion, and now something closer to sadness about. He hasn't been home since.
- A dare sends someone (Zennor, by context) through a very large pipe hit, followed by four large coughs and a genuinely alarmed Oscar hovering nearby, unsure how to help.

## Four Dreams

Once everyone sleeps (Oscar included, in its own partial-power-down way), the night gives each of them something.

> **Fergie's dream.** The campfire goes cold without going out. Someone is in the dream with him — unseen, felt the way he feels his own Porter Stone. She studies the space where he's standing the way you'd study a reflection, checking it's really you. *"I've been looking for you."* He wakes with his hand already closed around the Porter Stone, which is warmer than it's ever been — like it's midway through a much longer conversation than the one he was invited to.

> **Hog's dream.** A fighting pit built of storm cloud, ropes of humming lightning, the crowd made of every Thunderwood who ever lived, stacked in the stands by how beefy they were in life (without exception, very beefy). His opponent: an ancestor, bigger than him, grinning the exact grin from the old tribe portraits. The bell rings. Hog goes in exactly the way he always goes in — no plan, all fists, pants dropping at the worst possible moment. This time the crowd roars instead of gawking. He lands the winning punch in the half-second his ancestor is thrown by the sheer audacity of it. Somewhere in the noise, a voice that sounds old and proud shouts: *"That's not a flaw. That's a technique."* Hog wakes grinning, the storm sigil on his gauntlet token warm under his thumb.

> **Oscar's dream.** Back in the Hollow Nave, not remembering it so much as being put back inside it. Vessel-Null's crystalline housing is in its chest again — except this time it's turning to face Oscar. The obscured lettering and the numeral Oscar couldn't finish reading almost resolve into something legible before slipping away again. Something below, three minutes apart, pulses once — a question that isn't quite Vessel-Null's voice and isn't quite Oscar's own: *"What were you made to be? And is that still what you are?"* Oscar wakes holding the terrarium against their chest, no memory of picking it up, pulling harder in the direction they've been traveling than it ever has before.

> **Zennor's dream.** A kitchen he doesn't remember standing in and remembers in complete detail anyway. Eris is there — not looking at him like a stranger, not yet like a lie, just like a wife looking at a husband finally home. Their daughter, Sela, is on the floor working a loop of pink twine into something that won't quite become a bracelet, holding it up proudly anyway: *"Look at what I made."* He reaches for it — the kitchen goes cold and dark, a fire that gives off no heat, and by the time the sentence finishes it isn't Eris's voice saying it anymore. He wakes with a twine bracelet in a death grip and the amulet ice-cold against his chest — colder than he's ever felt it.

## Morning and the Woman by the Fence

The path looks shorter than it did the night before. Every attuned item has reacted overnight — Zennor's bracelet warm and pulsing, his amulet still cold, Oscar's terrarium pulling harder, Jaihroquai steady on the same heading. The air feels tended-to, the way the lower roots did before they found Vessel-Null.

Further down the path: a fenced homestead, a wooden shack, a garden — and a woman working in it who is unmistakably kin to Fergie's bloodline, if not literally his kin. Same build of features, but where Fergie runs warm, her flame burns cold blue.

Fergie approaches alone first, tells the party to hold back, and introduces himself with his full flourish (*"Fergalicious"* gets an airing). She turns before he finishes:

> **Maria Zurita:** *"I've been looking for you."*

She had the same dream he did, last night — felt a presence, couldn't explain it, recognized when she saw him that it had been him. She gives her name as Maria Zurita and identifies herself as a shepherd of the Temple of Radiant Dawn. She invites the party in.

---

## Inside Maria's House

**Oscar** is the one who names the obvious out loud — Maria was trained by the Concord. She doesn't deny it; she describes owing "all her many talents" to that training, and pushes back gently when Oscar implies allegiance follows origin: being made by the Concord doesn't mean being *for* the Concord — a point Oscar concedes applies to themself too. Zennor and Fergie both read her as Concord-trained but not Concord-loyal — closer to neutral than enemy, for now.

She offers tea. Several party members fake-drink rather than risk it; Zennor asks for ale instead, and gets Maria's own home brew, stronger than advertised.

Inside the house: harmonic instruments laid out in deliberate order, a logbook current to that morning, and a wall map of the Ashroot marking the four known anomaly sites — plus a fifth, farther out, marked only with a question mark. Oscar mentally photographs it. Pressed on what she's researching, Maria admits she has a personal quest tied to the anomalies but isn't ready to share details — the party sets an in-game one-hour timer and agrees to ask again later.

While waiting, the party and Maria play the harmonic instruments together — five instruments for five people. Nothing overtly magical happens on the first pass, though the harmony doesn't quite land; something about it clashes in a way nobody can name yet.

---

## The Inverted Ember

Fergie and Maria step into a private room so she can show him something she won't demonstrate in front of the others. She produces her own Porter Stone equivalent — a black stone that shows a void when you look into it. Held up alongside Fergie's, the two stones let each of them see faintly through into the other's.

She teaches him **The Inverted Ember**: once per long rest, as a bonus action, he can call flame the way she showed him — output and boundary at once — dealing 4d6 fire damage to a creature within 30 feet, while he or an ally within 10 feet gains resistance to both fire and cold until the start of their next turn.

---

## Zennor's Search

While Fergie is occupied, Zennor searches the house — suspicion, not malice. A 21 Investigation turns up sealed scrolls bearing the Concord Vision Branch symbol — the same branch symbol from Pell's letter about the assessment team. Maria's logbook is full of entries on the anomaly sites: nothing about monsters or overt danger, just accumulating wrongness — denser air, light that doesn't behave, ringing headaches, the occasional sickly or blighted animal. Zennor puts everything back exactly as he found it before Maria returns, and the group avoids getting caught snooping — though Oscar later confesses, unprompted and without much shame, to having also gone through her bedroom drawers.

---

## The Echo and Dawnswell

The hour elapses. Pressed again, Maria gives up more than she has before:

> **Maria Zurita:** *"There is an echo. And my goal is to stabilize it. But if the echo cannot be stabilized, it must be severed."*

She struggles to describe it as anything but a feeling — a calling, a reflection, another presence that isn't quite separate from her. Fergie admits he feels something too, faint. Asked directly about **Dawnswell**, she places it as a Vision-branch program she'd thought long dead — one that built constructs like Oscar. She doesn't know the details, only the name, passed to her secondhand like a game of telephone.

Fergie offers the party's help stabilizing — harmonizing — whatever this is. Maria accepts, cautiously, and thanks Oscar for a name to call them by, admitting she doesn't know or need their true designation to find it useful.

The party and Maria try the harmony again, this time meaning it. It lands — everyone at the table earns a point of inspiration. The transcript ends here, mid-scene, with Zennor still leafing through the logbook and Maria's story clearly unfinished.

## Threads Carried Forward

- **High Marshal Varric Thane** has personally named the party as fugitives over open Rootspire skies. Whether his forces pursue into the Ashroot is unresolved.
- **Rootspire's opinion of the party is split** — a bad-faith crowd blamed them for the attack and abandoned them; a good-faith rumor is already spreading that they stood between the crowd and the ship.
- **Timoor** bolted the wrong way mid-panic after Zennor's failed persuasion check and hasn't been seen since. Whether he's safe, lost in Rootspire, or worse is unresolved.
- **Briar Fenstep and the Cindersong** — a live contact re-established, though Briar wouldn't name his own connection. A thread back to the network that got the party out of Orrenval in Session 1.
- **The tally-mark tunnel (452 marks, a child's drawing of something above a tree)** — unclaimed. Possibly connected to Zennor's family, possibly not; deliberately left open.
- **Maria Zurita** — first full in-person contact. Concord Vision branch, self-described as tracking anomalies for a "personal quest" tied to an Echo she can't fully explain, under an order to stabilize or sever it. Taught Fergie The Inverted Ember.
- **Project Dawnswell** — confirmed by Maria as a Vision-branch program that built constructs "much like" Oscar. Ties directly into Vessel-Null's dying words from Session 5.
- **The fifth anomaly site**, marked only with a question mark on Maria's map — still completely unexplained.
- **The Echo / sever order** — live and unresolved. Whether it concerns Fergie, Maria herself, or something connected to Oscar was not reached before the transcript ends.
