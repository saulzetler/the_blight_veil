## Must-Hit Beats

> [!important] Must Hit
> **① Fear curdles into pressure to leave.** Grounded in Boughwarden Thalan's already-established dread of exactly this happening — not a moral judgment on the party, a survival instinct from a city that has spent years trying not to be noticed.

> [!important] Must Hit
> **② The party is offered a genuine three-way choice of exit** — market, rope bridges, or hidden tunnel — with no combat in any of them.

> [!important] Must Hit
> **③ Whichever route they pick sets the direction Maria's house is approached from in Scene 05.** Note it now; you'll need it later.

---

## DM Prep Notes

> [!warning] DM Only
> - **Keep this grey, not villainous.** Thalan and the crowd are not wrong to be afraid — that's the entire point. Rootspire has spent years staying off the Concord's radar and just watched that fail publicly. Play the backlash as fear, not malice.
> - **No combat, under any circumstances.** If a player tries to fight the crowd, redirect hard — a shove, a scramble, nothing that draws blood. This is a social and logistical obstacle, not a fight.
> - **Don't let this scene run past 20 minutes.** It exists to get the party moving with urgency, not to relitigate their choices from prior sessions.
> - **Whichever exit they choose is a real decision with a real consequence later** — see the route table below. Don't railroad toward one; present all three with equal weight and let them decide fast, since the pressure of the moment should make deliberation feel risky.

## Running the Scene

> [!quote] Read Aloud — The City Turns
> The square that greeted you an hour ago is unrecognisable. Stalls overturned, a child crying somewhere out of sight, someone dragging a cart of belongings toward the lower gate as if the ship might return any second.
>
> And people are looking at you. Not everyone — but enough. A vendor who sold you bread yesterday won't meet your eyes now. A gatewarden's hand rests, pointedly, on the hilt of something.
>
> Then Thalan is there, moving through the crowd like it costs him something — broad-shouldered, grey hair braided through with thin root-fibers, his usual measured calm gone thin.

> [!quote] Read Aloud — Thalan
> *"I have spent years — years — keeping that ship out of our sky. You will not be the reason it comes back."*
>
> *(He doesn't wait for a response. He's already scanning the crowd, the rooftops, the exits.)*
>
> *"You need to be gone. Now. However you get out, get out — I cannot protect you here, and every minute you stay is a minute I have to explain to my own people why I let you."*

Let the party respond — argue, apologize, push back, comply. Thalan doesn't have much more to give them beyond urgency and, if pressed kindly, a genuine flicker of guilt:

> [!quote] Thalan — If Pressed
> *"I don't think you're what he says you are. I think it stopped mattering the moment he pointed at us. That's what power like his does — it doesn't ask if it's right."*

## The Three Ways Out

Present all three plainly and let the party pick. Whichever they choose becomes their travel direction for the rest of the session.

### Option A — Through the Rootwalk Market

> [!quote] Read Aloud
> The market is chaos — overturned stalls, panicked crowds, someone's pack animal loose and screaming. It's the fastest way to the outer gate, but it's also the most visible: dozens of eyes, any one of which could be reporting back to someone.

**Check:** DC 12 Athletics or Acrobatics to get the whole party through without incident. **On a failure:** nothing dangerous — just a scramble, someone grabs at Oscar's arm in fear before letting go, a moment of real ugliness that doesn't need to become violence.

**NPC voice — Bram, a bread-stall vendor** who sold the party breakfast yesterday, now refusing to look at them:
> *"I've got a family behind that curtain. I sold you bread because you paid me and you were polite about it. That's all it was. Don't make me regret it — just go."*

**If the crowd turns on Oscar specifically** (optional beat, line above):
> A woman grabs at his arm, not to hurt him, just to *stop* him — to make him the reason this is happening to somebody, anybody, other than her. *"Is it because of him? Look at him — that's not natural, that's not —"* She lets go the moment anyone pushes back, more frightened of her own outburst than of Oscar.

### Option B — Over the Canopy Spiral Rope Bridges

> [!quote] Read Aloud
> Up and out. Quieter than the market, but exposed — the rope bridges sway in open air, in clear view of anywhere the ship might still be watching. Slower going, but nobody down below is paying attention up here.

**Check:** DC 13 Acrobatics or Dexterity save crossing swaying bridges, half-distracted by scanning the sky for the ship's return. **On a failure:** a stumble, a dropped item, nothing worse — but a beat of real tension, hanging above the city that just turned on them.

**NPC voice — a gatewarden** stationed at the Spiral's lower anchor, not stopping them:
> *"I didn't see you come this way. Nobody did. That's the only help I've got left to give — the Marshal's people don't look up as much as they should."*

### Option C — Through a Cindersong Whisper-Tunnel

> [!quote] Read Aloud
> Someone has to know it's there — a hidden path built by rebel sympathizers decades ago, concealed in a knot-hollow most people walk past without a second look. Dark, close, and by far the safest from anyone watching from above.

**Check:** DC 12 Investigation or Survival to actually locate the entrance in the panic (an NPC — Briar Fenstep, a gatewarden, whoever you like — can point them toward it without a check if you want to guarantee this option is available). **On a failure:** lost time, nothing worse — they find it a few minutes later, more shaken than they'd like to admit.

**NPC voice — Briar Fenstep**, if he's the one who points them to it:
> *"My grandmother's grandmother dug part of this by hand, hiding from a different war. I never thought I'd be the one showing someone through it. Watch your heads, and don't touch the root-walls — some of them are still listening."*

## If / Then — Player Responses

| If the party... | Then... |
|---|---|
| Try to reason with the crowd at large | A few individuals soften, most don't. This is a fear response, not a debate — let one or two NPCs be moved, and let that be enough without changing the overall pressure to leave. |
| Try to protect a specific targeted local (e.g. Oscar) with force | Redirect away from violence — a firm shove, a raised voice, physical presence without harm. No blood in this scene. |
| Ask Thalan to come with them | He refuses — his place is here, with his city, cleaning up what's left. This can be a quietly sad beat if you want it. |
| Try to split up and take multiple routes | Let it happen if they insist, but it complicates the direction-setting for Scene 05 — simplest fix is to have the routes converge again just outside the treeline before nightfall. |

## Optional Flavor Bank

> [!tip] Optional — All Skippable
> Fear, not malice. Every line below should be playable as a person doing the wrong thing for a reason you'd recognise in yourself. If any of it starts sounding like a villain, cut it.

### The Crowd — Overheard, Unattributed

- *"They were at the trial. They were right there at the trial and we let them walk."*
- *"I'm not saying hand them over. I'm saying I'd understand if somebody did."*
- *"Whose city is this? Whose city is it actually?"*
- *"He said complicit. Do you know what that word means? I know what that word means."*
- *"They didn't do anything to us. They just — happened near us."*
- *"My sister's at the outer farms. She can't get in and I can't get out."*
- *"Don't touch them. Just — don't touch them, and don't talk to them, and let them go."*
- *"Somebody should say something to them. Somebody who isn't me."*

### Small Kindnesses — Use At Least One

*The scene lands wrong if every single face is closed. One of these keeps it grey.*

- A woman presses a wrapped half-loaf into the nearest PC's hands, says nothing, and walks off fast so nobody sees her do it.
- Someone has already chalked an arrow on a root-wall pointing toward the least-watched exit. It's fresh. Whoever drew it isn't around.
- A boy of about ten follows the party for two streets, out of curiosity rather than fear, until his mother hauls him back by the collar.
- Selune of the Knots pushes through the crowd, out of breath, and asks Oscar one question — *"Did the tree do anything? When it came. Did it do anything?"* — and looks devastated by whatever answer she gets.
- Nyris the Sapbrewer shoves two vials at whoever's closest. *"Sap-draught. Don't ask what's in it, I'm not in a mood. Go."*
- An old man holds a door open on a route the party isn't taking, just in case, and keeps holding it long after they've passed.

### The Ugly Side — Use Sparingly

- Someone has already scratched a word into a stall's shutter. It's short. It's about Oscar.
- A knot of four or five men are standing in the party's path, not blocking it exactly, just standing there, waiting to see what the party does about it. They move if pushed. They wanted to be pushed.
- A vendor the party bought from yesterday very deliberately turns their sign around.
- Somebody throws something. It's a piece of fruit and it misses, and the person who threw it looks immediately, visibly ashamed.

### Thalan — More Lines

- **If asked where they should go:** *"Away. I'm not being cruel. I genuinely do not want to know, because I will be asked, and I would rather not have to decide whether to lie."*
- **If a PC apologises:** *"Don't. If you apologise I have to accept it, and then I have to be the man who accepted an apology and threw you out anyway."*
- **If asked whether he believes Thane will come back:** *"Men like that always come back. The question is what he wants to find when he does."*
- **If asked what happens to Rootspire now:** *"We will have a meeting. It will go badly. Then we will have another one."*
- **If Oscar speaks to him directly:** *(He makes himself hold eye contact, which visibly costs him.)* *"I have no quarrel with you. I want that on the record, even if the only record is this street."*
- **The last thing he says, as they go:** *"If it helps — and it won't — I'd have made the same choice you did, in Orrenval. That's what frightens me."*

### Route Texture — Market

- The loose pack animal is a goat, and it is having the best day of its life.
- A stall of dried herbs has gone over and the whole run smells overwhelmingly of crushed thyme, which will be a strange thing to remember this day by.
- Someone's fighting to save a sack of grain while their neighbour's roof is on fire, and neither of them is wrong.
- Bram the bread vendor has his curtain drawn but his hand is visible at the edge of it, holding it shut.
- The scattered warforged figurines from Scene 01 are underfoot here. Somebody has stepped on one.
- A city guard makes eye contact, looks at the party's weapons, looks away, and lets them pass. He'll remember doing that.

### Route Texture — Canopy Spiral

- From up here the whole city's panic is visible at once, silent, like watching an anthill get kicked.
- The bridges are wet with the morning still on them. Nobody has walked these today.
- Someone left a shoe on the third bridge. Just the one.
- The scorch mark from the first warning shot is directly below at one point in the crossing. From this angle you can see it went straight through two floors.
- Wind up here carries voices from the lower tiers in fragments — a word or two at a time, none of them good ones.
- The gatewarden at the lower anchor doesn't watch them go. He watches the sky the entire time, which is its own kind of answer.

### Route Texture — Cindersong Tunnel

- The knot-hollow entrance is behind a stack of firewood that has clearly been restacked in the same wrong way for forty years.
- Inside, the root walls are carved with tally marks. Hundreds of them. Nobody's counted.
- The air is warm and close and smells of old smoke, though nothing has burned down here in living memory.
- Someone has left a lantern hanging at the first junction, oil topped up, wick trimmed. Recently.
- The tunnel muffles everything. Twenty feet in, the city stops existing.
- There's a child's drawing scratched at knee height about halfway along — a tree, a sun, and a shape above the tree that is not a bird.
- A section of root-wall pulses faintly when touched, which is either the tree or the tunnel or nothing at all.

### Oscar's Sensor Readouts

- *Ambient: 71 dB, distributed. Crowd. No coordination in the signal — this is not a mob yet.*
- *Vocal stress markers elevated across 90% of sampled speakers. Assessment: fear.* (Pause.) *Not anger. Fear.*
- *Tunnel ambient: 9 dB. This is the quietest place I have measured since activation.*
- *Bridge ambient: wind at 40 dB, constant. Voices from below, intermittent, 30 dB. I can hear individual words. I would rather not.*
- *Reading discarded. It was of someone crying and I do not think that should be a number.*
