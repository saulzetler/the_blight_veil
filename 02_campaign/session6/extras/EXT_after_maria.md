## A — She Asks for the Night

Picks up directly from `06_cliffhanger`'s "Come with us" answer — *"Ask me again when I've had a night."* If nobody presses past that, and nobody's in a hurry to leave, she doesn't rescind the house.

> [!quote] Read Aloud
> She doesn't offer beds so much as point at them — the chair that's held someone's shape, the floor, a second room nobody's mentioned until now. She's already turning back to the hearth before anyone can answer.
>
> *"I have four hundred and six days of practice sleeping in a house that doesn't have guests in it. Tonight it does. I don't know what to do with that, so I'm going to go find out."*

**One thing happens overnight — pick one, not both:**

- **The fourth dispatch tube.** Three of the four sealed Vision tubes on her desk were already open (`05_maria.md`'s Environmental Detail Bank). Tonight, in front of anyone still awake, she opens the fourth. It's routine — a resupply notice, nothing about the party — but her hands shake reading it anyway, and she doesn't explain why until asked twice.
- **The margin note, once more.** Whoever noticed *"still here today"* crossed out eleven times in her logbook can watch her write it a twelfth, tonight, in front of them, and not cross it out.

**Skill Check — Insight, DC 14:** She's not deciding whether to trust the party. She's deciding whether she's allowed to want them to stay past tonight.

### Ending Here

> [!quote] Closing Line
> Morning comes the way it did the night before — grey, quiet, unremarkable — except this time there are four extra cups on her table, washed, and she's already put them away.

## B — The Road Has Company

Picks up if the party leaves immediately and there's still runtime to spend. Converges with Secrets & Clues Bank items #5 and #10 from `00_outline.md`: Concord Vision agents in traveling clothes have been asking after "a young Genasi woman, blue flame," and one of them tore a name off a Rootspire notice board.

> [!quote] Read Aloud
> An hour out from the house, the trees thin at a bend in the path — and there's a fire already lit that isn't yours. Two travelers, plain clothes, no armor visible, a kettle on. One of them stands slowly as you come into the light. Not a hand near a weapon. Just standing.
>
> *"Didn't expect company this far out. You wouldn't happen to have passed a house back that way — timber, one shuttered window?"*

They are Concord Vision, not Might — quiet, procedural, not looking for a fight. They don't know Maria by name, only by description. Whether the party lies, stonewalls, or gives her up is entirely theirs to choose; none of it undoes what already happened at the house.

| Check | DC | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Deception (denying they've seen anyone) | 13 | They're believed, for now — but one agent lingers a beat too long on Oscar before moving on. |
| Insight (reading the agents) | 12 | These two are tired, methodical, and not enjoying this assignment. Not zealots. |
| Persuasion (redirecting them elsewhere) | 14 | They take the bait and head the wrong way — buys real time, not permanent safety. |

> [!warning] DM Only
> Don't let this become combat. If the party threatens them, they de-escalate and withdraw — they're not equipped or inclined to fight a group that just survived an airship. Whatever the party tells them becomes a live thread for Session 7: word gets back to whoever Maria answers to, on a timeline the party doesn't control.

### Ending Here

> [!quote] Closing Line
> The agents' fire is a long way behind you before anyone speaks again. Nobody asks whether it was the right call. Nobody's sure it was.

## C — If It Goes Loud

Only for the specific case where the table doesn't hold at the cut-to-black in `06_cliffhanger` — an attack, or cornering her past the point she can talk her way out. Her full block is `03_characters/npcs/maria_zurita.md`'s **Acolyte-level stat block (CR 3)** — not the Echo-Awakened Seer. Keep this short: three rounds, not a dungeon fight.

**Her intent:** disengage, not kill. She's controlled Concord Seer training, not a soldier, and she doesn't want this any more than the party should want it.

- **Round 1:** Her Porter Stone flares inverted-cold. She calls out — once — for it to stop, and it isn't a threat, it's closer to a plea.
- **Round 2:** If it doesn't stop, she defends only — no offensive spellcasting aimed to kill, just enough control-branch effects to create distance (a burst of frost-fog for concealment, a shove of cold air to knock someone back).
- **Round 3:** She's gone — through the same sealed root-gap or a side door, however you want to stage her exit. She does not die tonight. This isn't negotiable; it breaks Fergie's entire arc if she does.

> [!quote] If She Gets a Last Line Before She Goes
> *"I didn't come here for this."* *(And then, quieter, to Fergie specifically, if he's close enough to hear it:)* *"Neither did you, I think."*

### Ending Here

> [!quote] Closing Line
> The house is empty. The fire's still burning, making no sound, the way it never has. Nobody moves to put it out.

## D — Sitting With It

For a table that's emotionally done but not ready for the abruptness of the written cut-to-black. A quiet coda, no new information, no new threat — just processing, on the road or back at a camp.

> [!quote] Read Aloud
> Nobody suggests stopping. Somebody stops anyway, and then everybody does, because it turns out that was the thing everyone was waiting for permission to do.

**Offer one prompt to the table, then get out of the way:**
- Who says her name first, and who flinches when they hear it?
- Does Oscar say anything about the terrarium, or does everyone just watch it, still leaning?
- Is anyone angry at Fergie for how he played it? Is anyone angry at themselves for how they didn't?

### Ending Here

> [!quote] Closing Line
> The fire burns down to coals. Nobody's first watch. Nobody's decided who's taking it.
